Toronto has implemented new Major Transit Station Areas which are spaces around transit stations designated for future growth, employment and housing that have become the premise of this studio. Anchored by the Gerrard–Carlaw MTSA intersection, my concept begins with a straightforward strategy: introduce infill buildings with improved setbacks, establish inter-block connections through enhanced or newly created laneways, and activate underused rear-yard spaces.

This approach has the potential to reshape land-use planning so that mid-block corridors function as formalized public connections—suggesting for example, proposing to enable greater density when a parcel provides public easements—for example, Simpson Avenue could evolve into an interior pedestrian thoroughfare on private property.

Situated across a three-lot assembly on Simpson Avenue, the project explores an architectural language of south-facing terraces stepping down toward Gerrard Street. This massing establishes both a threshold and a porous mid-block passage, drawing pedestrians from the perimeter streets into an active, rear-block community.
