Encapsulated between the longhouses and forest at Crawford Lake, this museum represents a body for light, memory, and spatial experience, expressed through the language of double-curvature forms and natural materials. From the moment visitors arrive at the parking lot, a path guides them toward the entrance, where a gentle lift of wooden slats marks the transition from the exterior landscape to the museum’s interior world. Upon entry, visitors are greeted by a grandiose atrium, one of two, that subtly echoes the form and spirit of the longhouses at Crawford Lake.

Double-curvature forms continue to shape the spatial and formal language of the museum. Like the entrance, the atrium is wrapped in another lifting screen of wooden slats, creating an organic façade that responds to both light and shadow. Skylights punctuate the roofline, lifting upwards in a similar gesture. Moreover, this project is an exploration of how form, light, and cultural significance can come together to create meaningful experience. Through the architecture’s curved forms, material language, and choreographed spatial sequence, the museum aspires to become a space that is not just visited, but experienced and remembered.
